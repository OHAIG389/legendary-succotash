Atomic Weapons

In Planet of the Apes (1968), they make many notably course connections, notably including the Atomic Bomb. In our class, we learned about nuclear weapons and their unintended effects, which are displayed prominently in this movie. Throughout the movie, Taylor is motivated by his desire to find out where he has landed, and his friends, Cornelius and Zira, help show him the true history of this world. At the end of the movie, Taylor finds the remains of the Statue of liberty, revealing that this planet is actually Earth, and that humans have destroyed themselves from nuclear warfare. This movie was released during the height of the Cold War, when people across the world lived in fear of Mutually Assured Destruction. The course mentions hydrogen and atomic bombs as "... the latest symbol of ... the geopolitical insecurity of the cold war" (Cowan 235)

Space and Space Travel

The movie also heavily features the use of spaceships, as the crew use their space ships as a way to travel through space and through time. The Space Race was also mentioned in the textbook, as it was a competition between the global superpowers of the United States and the Soviet Union. The textbook focuses on space as it is literally one of the places that we knew very little about, and how it was used as a propaganda tool by both countries to claim superiority over the others. In the real world, the space race started with the launch of Sputnik, to which the US stated their claim of landing a man on the moon. The movie was released a year before Apollo 11 landed on the moon, and it reflected humanities curiousness towards the stars. It literally is a movie about space travel and exploration, which is one of the defining topics of the '60s. The author reflects this belief, as they state "... President Kennedy announced that the United States ... was going to land a man on the moon. There was some, but not very much, scientific value in such an enterprise. Kennedy's motives were purely political; he was determined to beat the Soviets at what had become by then their own space game" (Cowan 235)

Science and Opression

Both the textbook and the movie discuss the theme of science and those who oppose important scienctific discoveries. The movie centers around the head apes hiding evidence that humans were the intelligent species, and that apes were the dumb, uncivilized ones, and the textbook, which uses the examples of master carpenters and their replacement by machines. They both connect back the to theme of those who hold pwoer do not want to lose that power. Specifically, the author says, "After the Civil War, the onslaught of the machines began, and the shaky ladder collapsed completely." and later, "... subcontracting masters could be dispensed with also, and builders could employ the unskilled or semiskilled laborers rather than the (considerably more expensive) skilled artisans" (Cowan 141) The apes do not want the truth revealed, as it means that they lied, and that the sapian civilization only rose following the destruction of the human race, and it turns out different than the example of the master carpenter. The master carpenters controlled the manufacturing of wooden objects throughout many generations, and they took advantage of that by charging large amounts of money, and by paying journeyman and apprentice carpenters very little. That was, until, industrialization and mechanization made them obsolete, because advanced machines enabled unskilled workers to make beautiful, and expensive, wooden objects and furniture. Mechanization reduced the need for skilled labor, and replaced it with cheaper and more efficient factory labor.
